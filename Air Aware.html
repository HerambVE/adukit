GGreen light: full tipsGood air · AQI 0–100

What it means

Pollution is low and the air is safe for everyone. Very sensitive people, such as those with asthma, may notice a little discomfort near the top of this range.

What you can do

  • Play, walk, cycle or have class outdoors.
  • Open windows to let fresh air in.
  • Walk or cycle short trips, and never burn leaves or garbage, to keep the air green.
YYellow light: full tipsFairly good · AQI 101–200

What it means

Okay for most healthy people, but pollution is building up. People with asthma, lung or heart problems, young children and older adults may feel it.

What you can do

  • Take it easy with long or hard outdoor exercise.
  • Stay away from busy roads and rush hour.
  • If you have asthma, keep your inhaler with you.
  • Don't add to it: share rides, skip firecrackers, use less incense indoors.
RRed light: full tipsHazardous · AQI 201–500
Get medical help for breathlessness, chest pain, dizziness, or a cough that won't stop.

What it means

Most people may feel breathing discomfort if they stay out long. At the highest levels even healthy people are affected, and it's serious for children, older adults, pregnant women and anyone with asthma, lung or heart disease.

What you can do

  • Stay indoors and play indoor games.
  • Keep doors and windows closed.
  • If you must go out, wear a well-fitted N95 or N99 mask. Cloth masks don't filter tiny particles well.
  • Use an air purifier if you have one, and don't burn anything indoors or out.

iWhat can the model measure?The sensor, its limits, and India's AQI scale

Our sensor smells gases and smoke, such as ammonia, alcohol, benzene and carbon dioxide. India's official AQI is worked out from eight pollutants, including tiny dust particles (PM2.5 and PM10), using lab-grade machines. So our number is a clue about the air nearby, great for learning, but not a replacement for official readings.

Designed and built by young inventors who want everyone to breathe easy.

The Air Aware model uses an MQ135 gas sensor, an Arduino Uno, a 16×2 LCD screen and traffic-light LEDs. This website is a guide and isn't connected to the model. The lights follow the bands of India's National Air Quality Index (CPCB). Device readings are an estimate. Advice on this page is general guidance based on public health advisories, not medical advice. If you have a health condition, follow your doctor's advice.
